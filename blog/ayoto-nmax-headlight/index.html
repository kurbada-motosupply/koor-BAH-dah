AYOTO NMAX headlamp.

The listing title names a Yamaha NMAX V1 and V2 bi-LED headlamp from AYOTO. The seller photo is printed “NMAX NEW” and “APP CONTROL.” Open the product and match that body before you order.

The written listing calls it a full-set headlamp for the new NMAX, with a socket-to-socket plug. It names a high beam and a low beam, demon eyes, a running turn signal, and a 3-step switch on the mirror. The same text also prints more than one lamp size and more than one watt figure. Those lines are the seller’s copy. The variant you select on the product page is the one you are buying.

What it fits

  • Yamaha NMAX, the V1 and V2 bodies named in the title.
  • The front headlamp, as a full set. It is not a turn-signal-only part.

An Aerox lamp, a Click lamp, and a BeAT lamp are different listings. NMAX Connected and later bodies still need the year on the OR/CR. If the photo says NMAX NEW and your panels are an older shape, stop and ask the seller.

Check this before you open the product

  • The side panel says NMAX, and you know whether the body is V1 or V2.
  • You read the variant for color. The listing names demon-eye colors, including red, blue, purple, and ice blue, in different lines.
  • You can put the stock lamp back. The seller says the new unit plugs in without cutting the old one.
  • You treat the phone-control line in the title as something to confirm on the variant. The photo says app control. The spec list also describes a 3-step switch.

Road use

An aftermarket headlamp is not automatically approved for a public road. Beam aim, glare, and local rules still apply. This catalog does not certify the lamp. The seller’s brightness claims stay on the listing.
